Farflung Answers Today: Expedition 53

Farflung is a free daily geography game: five prompts, one country pinned for each, and the rarer your answer the further it carries you. This page lists the top-tier answer for every prompt in today's expedition and the rarity split for each card — because a new expedition opens at midnight in your own time zone, so which day you are on depends on where you are.

Today's expedition — #53, 26 November 2026

Game date 2026-11-26. If it is still 25 November where you live, you are on Expedition #52.

Prompt 1: Pin a country where manatees live in the wild

Gem, 100 km: the United Kingdom, Niger, the Netherlands and Chad — four countries share the top tier.

44 countries are accepted here — 1 Common, 4 Uncommon, 18 Rare, 17 Elusive and four sharing the top tier. The footnote decides this card, not the map: all three manatee species count, a country can qualify through a territory (France, by way of French Guiana), and recent sightings count too, which is how Curaçao gets in. A dugong does not count despite looking the part. That breadth is why the accepted list runs to 44 countries, and why the top tier is four wide. Niger and Chad sit in the African manatee's range, along the Niger River and the Lake Chad basin; the United Kingdom and the Netherlands are the two answers almost nobody types, and they are worth a full tier each. On a card this forgiving, the player who names one obvious tropical country and stops has already lost the expedition.

Prompt 2: Pin a country that signed the final communiqué of the 1955 Bandung conference

Gem, 100 km: Liberia.

28 countries are accepted — 1 Common, 2 Uncommon, 10 Rare, 14 Elusive and Liberia alone at the top. The arithmetic is the first clue: 29 delegations were in the room, and two of them were the Vietnam of the day, North and South, which fold into one country on a modern map. Ghana is accepted under the name it used then, the Gold Coast, and delegations that attended as observers, Cyprus among them, do not count at all. Liberia is the answer almost nobody reaches for: a participant whose name leaves no trace in the photographs everyone remembers from the conference, and a full 100 km for anyone who thinks of it. With India and China at 55 km and Indonesia at 40 km, the three answers every player types first are the three worth least.

Prompt 3: Pin a country whose gymnast has won gold at the Summer Olympics

Gem, 100 km: Tajikistan, Azerbaijan, Kyrgyzstan and Turkmenistan — four countries share the top tier.

43 countries are accepted — 1 Common, 4 Uncommon, 13 Rare, 21 Elusive and four sharing the top tier. The footnote carries the hard cases: gymnasts who competed for the Soviet Union, the Unified Team, Czechoslovakia or Yugoslavia are credited to the country of their birth or of their upbringing, and a Youth Olympics gold does not count. That single rule is what puts four Caucasus and Central Asian republics at the top — a Soviet gold can belong to a republic that never fielded its own gymnastics team before 1992 — while the countries everyone names first sit underneath: China, Romania, Russia and Japan at 55 km, the United States at 40 km. Remembering one famous champion is worth less here than recalling where the Soviet team's gymnasts were born.

Prompt 4: Pin a country that has had a World Barista Champion

Gem, 100 km: El Salvador.

16 countries are accepted — 1 Common, 2 Uncommon, 7 Rare, 5 Elusive and El Salvador alone at the top. One winner a year keeps this the shortest accepted list of the five, and the rule is that a champion counts for the country whose national barista title they held, not the country they were born in. Australia at 40 km is the answer the card treats as the default; the United Kingdom, Japan, the United States, Denmark, South Korea, Norway and Indonesia fill the 70 km tier. El Salvador is the pin worth memorising, and the card's own footnote points at the other surprise: Italy, the country that gave the world espresso, has never produced a champion.

Prompt 5: Pin a country that has had a Panda Express restaurant

Gem, 100 km: Russia and the Netherlands — two countries share the top tier.

13 countries are accepted — 1 Common, 3 Uncommon, 5 Rare, 3 Elusive and two sharing the top tier. The footnote does the work again: restaurants that have since closed still count, which is exactly how Russia qualifies, and China has none — a Kunming outlet that opened in 2020 was a hoax, so the one country everyone assumes must be on the list is not on it. The United States at 40 km is the home market and the first answer anybody types; Canada and Mexico follow at 55 km; Japan, the Philippines, South Korea, the United Arab Emirates and Saudi Arabia fill 70 km. Germany, Guatemala and El Salvador at 85 km and the two at the top are the pins to keep.

How the five tiers score

A correct pin adds kilometres, and how many depends on how rare your country is. The tiers run Common 40 km, Uncommon 55 km, Rare 70 km, Elusive 85 km and Farflung 100 km, so a perfect five-gem expedition is 500 km. A pin that does not fit the prompt scores zero. These figures come from the stored daily expedition record; the official how-to-play page names the five tiers but does not publish the kilometre values.

Tiers are judged per prompt, not per country, and ties change the arithmetic. Two of today's five cards are won by a tie — four countries on the manatee card and four on the gymnastics card — so a player who memorises one answer per prompt lands in the middle rather than at the top. Meanwhile the Netherlands is at the top of two different cards in the same expedition, and El Salvador is the single 100 km gem on the barista card and only 85 km on the Panda Express card twenty minutes later. There is no list of "rare countries" to learn; the only way to score well is to read each card literally and pick the country that fits it in the least expected way.

Official example, not from the daily game: for "a country where German is an official language", Germany is Common, Austria Uncommon, Liechtenstein Rare, Belgium Elusive and Luxembourg Farflung, because German is co-official there alongside French and Luxembourgish.

Three things that trip people up

  • Territories count for their parent country — with the game's own exceptions. Greenland scores as Denmark and Guam as the United States. But the game has folded and unfurled constituent countries differently from card to card, so read the card's footnote instead of assuming: today's manatee card credits France through French Guiana while the Bandung card keeps the Gold Coast under its own name.
  • One attempt per expedition. Leaving mid-round is allowed and you can come back, but the 60-second clock keeps running while you are away, and a timed-out prompt scores nothing.
  • 197 countries are in play — 193 UN members plus Vatican City, Palestine, Kosovo and Taiwan. Every prompt has at least one answer in the top tier, which is why a strange-looking answer is usually worth more than an obvious one.

When does a new Farflung expedition open?

At midnight in your own local time. Everyone gets the same five prompts for a given date, so two players in different time zones can be on different expedition numbers at the same moment.

Can I play today's expedition a second time?

No. One attempt per daily expedition. Practice mode gives you a single repeated prompt with no clock, so you can learn the map controls without touching your daily score.

Do I need an account to play Farflung?

No. Farflung runs free in a browser on a phone or a computer, as a guest, with nothing to download.

Is there a Farflung unlimited mode?

Not on the official game. Themed packs come first and an unlimited mode follows later. You can replay the past week from the in-game menu.

Where are the answers for older expeditions?

On the archive page, one page per date, newest first. The game itself only shows the current round, so finished expeditions survive only on pages like 25 November 2026.

Is farflunganswer.com the official Farflung site?

No. This is an independent fan site tracking the daily answers. The official game is published by Farflung LLC at farflung.game.
