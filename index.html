Farflung Answers Today: Expedition 54

Farflung is a free daily geography game: five prompts, one country pinned for each, and the rarer your answer the further it carries you. This page lists the top-tier answer for every prompt in today's expedition and the rarity split for each card — because a new expedition opens at midnight in your own time zone, so which day you are on depends on where you are.

Today's expedition — #54, 27 November 2026

Game date 2026-11-27. If it is still 26 November where you live, you are on Expedition #53.

Prompt 1: Pin a country whose flag contains a triangle

Gem, 100 km: fifteen countries share the top tier — Antigua and Barbuda, Burundi, Comoros, Djibouti, Eritrea, Equatorial Guinea, Grenada, Saint Kitts and Nevis, the Marshall Islands, Nicaragua, Solomon Islands, El Salvador, São Tomé and Príncipe, Tuvalu and Vanuatu.

47 countries are accepted — 1 Common, 2 Uncommon, 10 Rare, 19 Elusive and fifteen sharing the top tier. This is the widest top tier the game has put on a card, and the footnote is why: a triangle formed by a diagonal stripe, a cross or a serrated edge counts, so the triangle tucked into a Union Jack canton counts, and so do the triangle arms of Nicaragua and El Salvador. Government, military and naval ensigns are excluded, which is what trips people up — plenty of countries fly a flag with a triangle in a state version and never in the civil one. With fifteen answers at the top and only one at the bottom, naming a famous flag is almost certainly the wrong move.

Prompt 2: Pin a country that had a university before 1500

Gem, 100 km: twelve countries share the top tier — Ireland, Armenia, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, North Macedonia, Slovakia, Uzbekistan, Albania, Indonesia, North Korea, Algeria and Yemen.

41 countries are accepted — 1 Common, 2 Uncommon, 11 Rare, 15 Elusive and twelve sharing the top tier. The footnote opens the door both ways: institutions that have since closed still count, and the ancient seats of learning that were never formally chartered as universities count too, which is how Nalanda and its contemporaries put Bangladesh and India on the list. The countries everyone types first are all here but worth less — Italy, France and the United Kingdom sit mid-table. The top tier is a map of the medieval scholarly world that has nothing to do with modern university rankings: Bologna in 1088 is not the reference point, Timbuktu and Nalanda are.

Prompt 3: Pin a country where sun bears live in the wild

Gem, 100 km: Brunei and Bangladesh — two countries share the top tier.

11 countries are accepted — 1 Common, 2 Uncommon, 4 Rare, 2 Elusive and two sharing the top tier. This is the shortest list of the day, so every answer is either the obvious one or a full tier. Borneo alone gives three separate pins — Malaysia, Indonesia and Brunei — because the game credits the country, not the island, and Brunei is a 100 km answer for a range that runs through a sliver of one state. Bangladesh is the other one: the sun bear's western limit, an animal almost nobody pictures when they think of the country. Singapore is the trap in the other direction: it has no wild sun bears left, so naming the region's most famous city-state scores nothing.

Prompt 4: Pin a country Bad Bunny has performed in on his 2025–26 tour

Gem, 100 km: five countries share the top tier — Australia, the Netherlands, Belgium, Sweden and Poland.

21 countries are accepted — 1 Common, 2 Uncommon, 10 Rare, 3 Elusive and five sharing the top tier. The footnote settles both geography questions: the tour skipped the mainland United States but closed in Puerto Rico, which scores as the United States, and a London date scores as the United Kingdom. The obvious answers are cheap here — the United States at 40 km, and the Latin American dates everyone pictures at 55 and 70 km. The top tier is the European and Australian leg, which is the part of the itinerary a fan of the music rather than the tour schedule would never recall: Poland, Sweden, Belgium, the Netherlands and Australia each carry a full 100 km.

Prompt 5: Pin a country that has had a FamilyMart store

Gem, 100 km: North Korea alone at the top.

11 countries are accepted — 1 Common, 2 Uncommon, 4 Rare, 3 Elusive and North Korea alone at the top. Japan at 40 km is the answer the card treats as the default, and Taiwan and South Korea at 55 km are the two every player types next. The footnote is the whole card: former stores count. South Korea's FamilyMarts were converted into CU in 2012, and the Fanima stores FamilyMart ran around Los Angeles closed in 2015, which is exactly why South Korea is still on the list at 55 km and the United States sits at 70 km with China, Thailand and the Philippines. North Korea is the answer nobody reaches for — a convenience-store chain inside the world's most closed economy — and it is worth a full tier.

How the five tiers score

A correct pin adds kilometres, and how many depends on how rare your country is. The tiers run Common 40 km, Uncommon 55 km, Rare 70 km, Elusive 85 km and Farflung 100 km, so a perfect five-gem expedition is 500 km. A pin that does not fit the prompt scores zero. These figures come from the stored daily expedition record; the official how-to-play page names the five tiers but does not publish the kilometre values.

Tiers are judged per prompt, not per country, and that is the lesson of today's set. Three of the five cards are won by a tie, one of them fifteen countries wide — on the flag card there is no single answer to memorise, only a category to understand, while on the FamilyMart card a single country takes the whole top tier. The same country can also score differently from card to card: Japan is the cheap default answer today at 40 km, and North Korea, which has almost no retail of any kind, is the 100 km pick.

Official example, not from the daily game: for "a country where German is an official language", Germany is Common, Austria Uncommon, Liechtenstein Rare, Belgium Elusive and Luxembourg Farflung, because German is co-official there alongside French and Luxembourgish.

Three things that trip people up

  • Territories count for their parent country — with the game's own exceptions. Greenland scores as Denmark and Guam as the United States, and today's Bad Bunny card credits Puerto Rico to the United States. But the game splits landmasses differently from card to card, so read the footnote instead of assuming: today's sun bear card gives Borneo three separate pins, one per country.
  • One attempt per expedition. Leaving mid-round is allowed and you can come back, but the 60-second clock keeps running while you are away, and a timed-out prompt scores nothing.
  • 197 countries are in play — 193 UN members plus Vatican City, Palestine, Kosovo and Taiwan. Every prompt has at least one answer in the top tier, which is why a strange-looking answer is usually worth more than an obvious one.

When does a new Farflung expedition open?

At midnight in your own local time. Everyone gets the same five prompts for a given date, so two players in different time zones can be on different expedition numbers at the same moment.

Can I play today's expedition a second time?

No. One attempt per daily expedition. Practice mode gives you a single repeated prompt with no clock, so you can learn the map controls without touching your daily score.

Do I need an account to play Farflung?

No. Farflung runs free in a browser on a phone or a computer, as a guest, with nothing to download.

Is there a Farflung unlimited mode?

Not on the official game. Themed packs come first and an unlimited mode follows later. You can replay the past week from the in-game menu.

Where are the answers for older expeditions?

On the archive page, one page per date, newest first. The game itself only shows the current round, so finished expeditions survive only on pages like 25 November 2026.

Is farflunganswer.com the official Farflung site?

No. This is an independent fan site tracking the daily answers. The official game is published by Farflung LLC at farflung.game.
